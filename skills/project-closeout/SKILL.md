# Project Closeout

## Workflow

1. Confirm the project or phase being closed and its applicable completion
   criteria. Accept user-provided scope, results, and evidence without requiring
   upstream skills.
2. Collect approved scope, delivered candidates and changes, verification,
   existing reviews, and user outcomes. Include acceptance and release status
   where applicable.
3. Reconcile expected and actual outcomes. Retain initial failures and work
   completed after reminders or rework. Distinguish verified behavior, untested
   behavior, missing historical evidence, and unresolved findings. When workflow
   quality is in scope, assess it separately from product functionality.
4. Assess record completeness against Output Contract and scoped work achievement
   against its applicable completion criteria. Do not convert unrun checks or
   planned releases into completed results. Record unresolved work and next-cycle
   inputs.
5. Return follow-up needs to the appropriate intake role when available and
   authorized; otherwise deliver the next-cycle inputs directly to the user.
   Report the actual handoff status.

## Record Preservation

- Unreported drafts may be revised. Preserve submitted approval material,
  approval records, reported results, and their key evidence as recoverable
  versions.
- Later repairs, retests, or corrections add distinguishable linked records
  in separate files or independently numbered sections. Preserve the earlier
  conclusion and its key evidence. One repair and retest round may share a record.
- These preservation obligations do not require a new record for every question,
  progress inquiry, or wording edit.
- Current-state documents and indexes may be updated while retaining historical
  entry points. New evidence cannot replace lost historical evidence.
- Preserve material needed to support decisions and conclusions; this does not
  require archiving every conversation or tool output.

## Boundaries

- A phase closeout does not establish whole-project acceptance or release;
  project completion requires evidence for all applicable required criteria.
- Record missing implementation self-checks, code repairs, professional audits,
  or release work and the responsible follow-up role; do not perform them to
  complete this closeout role.

## Output Contract

Include:

- Closeout scope, applicable completion criteria and their sources, and the
  delivered candidate's identity and location.
- A comparison containing: completion criterion; actual result; judgment;
  evidence and source; remaining action.
- Initial failures, work completed after reminders, and rework or retest
  history, with links between earlier and later records.
- Separate assessments of closeout record completeness and whether the
  scoped work meets its completion criteria.
- Next actions, responsible roles, follow-up entries, and actual handoff status.

## Format Selection and Completeness

Compare the existing project format against Output Contract before reading
a default template. If it can express the required information, or can
reasonably be supplemented to do so, reuse and complete it without loading
the default template.

Missing facts or evidence do not make a format unsuitable. Mark the missing
or unknown information and its impact in the chosen format. If no suitable
format can be reused or reasonably completed, load and use the reference
listed below.

Before delivery, check that every required item is represented, missing or
unknown information and its impact are explicit, and conclusions are supported
or limited by their cited sources. Attribute reused review conclusions without
claiming fresh verification. Distinguish designed cases from actual run
evidence, and user reports from agent-performed checks.

No particular directory or filename is required. Write headings, labels,
and content in the user's language.
